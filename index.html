MODEL BUILDING

Small Models, Big World

What Australian competition tables reveal about model subjects, scales and kit brands.

Harniq Singh · FIT3179 · 12 October 2026

1. What is on the workbench?

Model builders turn kits into miniature aircraft, vehicles, ships and imagined worlds. ScaleACT 2025 in Canberra recorded 817 model entries, including 259 aircraft. Each entry is a model, so one person can contribute several entries.

A scale of 1/48 means the model is one forty-eighth of the real object's size. For the same object, a 1/72 model is smaller than a 1/48 model.

2. A local hobby with global brands

The next three maps link competition entries to kit-company countries. 353 of 817 entries (43.2%) could be matched to 19 verified brands. The other 464 entries are excluded from these maps.

Company country means the country of the current company or owner. It does not identify where the kit was manufactured or where the builder lives.

Chart 2 · Where are the matched kit companies based?

Circle area shows the number of matched entries. Japan accounts for 169 of the 353 matches.

Chart 3 · How much of each country's matched total is aircraft?

Colour shows a percentage within each country. For Czechia, 38 of 39 matched entries are aircraft; for Japan, the figure is 42 of 169.

Grey means no matched entries or fewer than 10 matches. Percentages describe this matched sample, rather than a country's whole model industry.

Chart 4 · Overseas companies connect to a Canberra exhibition

Thicker lines represent more entries linked to that company country. The orange diamond marks Canberra, where the models were exhibited.

These are brand-to-exhibition connections. The lines are schematic, they do not measure shipping routes or the builders' journeys.
